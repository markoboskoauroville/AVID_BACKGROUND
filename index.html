BACKGROUND OFF
AVID MEDIA COMPOSER  /  v2 (a)

Field guide for editors

Stop the AI that is
eating your machine

Media Composer transcribes every clip in every bin, indexes every word anyone says in your project, and runs a separate transcode and render engine outside the application. All of it is on by default. On a busy newsroom workstation it competes with playback for the same CPU and the same disk, and you feel it as stutter, slow bin opening and lag on the timeline.

This guide explains what each background process actually does, then shows you every place it can be switched off, from a single bin all the way to a setting that applies to every project on the machine.

Part one

What is actually running

Six separate things, and they are not all the same animal. Two live inside Media Composer, two run as their own programs beside it, and two are system level. Knowing which is which tells you where to go to switch each one off.

PhraseFind AI indexing

Safe to stop

This is the big one. Since version 2023.8 Media Composer replaced the old phonetic search with real AI transcription that runs entirely on your machine, with no internet connection. When media arrives in a project, the indexer reads the audio of every clip and writes a full text transcript into a database, so that later you can type a word and jump to the moment somebody said it.

Avid states plainly in its own release notes that indexing is a resource intensive operation which may impact the performance of Media Composer. On a newsroom machine receiving fresh material all day, it never really finishes, because more media keeps arriving.

Feeds PhraseFind AI, ScriptSync AI and the Transcript Tool. If you never search for spoken words and never cut from a transcript, you are paying for something you do not use.

The transcript engine, bin by bin

Safe to stop

By default Media Composer will create a transcription for all media in a project. Avid acknowledges this is often wasteful, because music, sound effects and camera media without usable dialogue gain nothing from being transcribed. Every bin you leave included is more audio queued for processing.

From version 2024.6 onward you can include or exclude individual bins, and the Bin Container sidebar tells you the state of each one at a glance.

Controlled in three places: Project settings, the Transcript Settings window, and a right click on any bin.

Avid Background Services

Safe to stop

A separate program that starts with the computer and keeps running whether or not Media Composer is open. It is the engine behind background transcode, background consolidate, background render and Dynamic Media Folders. Anything you tick as a background job is handed to this service and chewed through while you edit.

You control it from an icon in the Windows taskbar or the macOS menu bar, not from inside Media Composer. The same icon opens the Background Queue window, which is where you find out whether something is silently working through a list of jobs right now.

Process name on Windows: AvidBackgroundServicesManager.exe. Formerly called Avid Editor Services Manager.

Dynamic Media Folders

Safe to stop

Watch folders. You point one at a directory, and anything dropped there is ingested and transcoded automatically against a profile, without anybody pressing a button. Useful in a dailies workflow, pure overhead if nobody set it up on purpose. It depends on Background Services, so stopping those stops this too.

Not available on macOS Catalina and later, so on a modern Mac this one is already off.

Avid Link

Check before stopping

Launches at login, sits in the background, handles your Avid account, product updates, marketplace feeds and notifications. It is the lightest of the six, but it is also the one most people are surprised to find running, because it offers no visible option to stop starting itself.

Warning: on subscription and floating licences, Avid Link is part of how the licence is checked. Do not remove it on a facility machine without asking your systems engineer first.

Avid Media Indexer

Leave alone on shared storage

Scans and catalogues media on storage so that editing systems can find it. On a standalone workstation with local drives it does very little. On a facility connected to NEXIS or MediaCentral it is load bearing, because it is how shared media gets discovered at all.

If your workstation sees shared workspaces, treat this as untouchable and let the engineering team decide.

Part two

Every place you can switch it off

Work outward, from the narrowest setting to the widest. The first four are ordinary settings inside Media Composer and are yours to change. The last three reach outside your project, so read the caution on each before you touch them on a station that is not your own.

In the
project

Stop new bins being enrolled, and pause while you cut

Two checkboxes here do most of the work, and they survive for the life of the project. The first stops transcription from stealing cycles at exactly the moment you need them, which is while your hands are on the keyboard. The second stops the pile of work growing every time somebody makes a bin.

  1. Open Settings then the Project tab, and choose Transcript.
  2. Tick Pause background transcription while editing. Transcription now halts as soon as you start working and only resumes after roughly ten seconds of inactivity.
  3. In the same Transcript Settings window, untick Include all new bins in transcription. New bins are no longer enrolled automatically.
Worth knowing. These are project settings, not application settings, so a brand new project starts with Avid's defaults again. The last step in this guide shows you how to make the choice stick for every future project.
In the
bin

Exclude the bins that will never need a transcript

Music, effects, graphics, mute camera material, archive and stock. None of it benefits from transcription, and all of it queues by default. Excluding a bin is instant and the sidebar labels the result, so anyone sitting down at the station can see the state without opening a menu.

  1. Right click the bin in the Bin Container sidebar.
  2. Choose Exclude Bin from Transcription.
  3. Check the label that appears to the right of the bin name. It reads Transcription Off when the bin is excluded.
  4. To put a bin back into the queue later, right click it again and choose Include Bin in Transcription.
Fastest habit to build. When you create a bin for music or effects, exclude it in the same breath as naming it. It costs one second and saves the machine an hour.
In the
find
window

Stop the indexer itself

This is the switch Avid points to in its own release notes when performance suffers, and it is the one most editors never find, because it hides behind a small gear in the bottom corner of a window nobody opens. Stopping the indexer halts transcription work without touching any other service.

  1. Go to Edit then Find, or press Ctrl+F on Windows and Cmd+F on macOS.
  2. Click the gear icon in the lower left corner to reach Search Settings.
  3. Choose Stop PhraseFind AI Indexing, not Pause. Stop holds until somebody deliberately starts it again. Pause only halts indexing temporarily and it will come back on you.
  4. Confirm it worked. The indicator beside PhraseFind AI Index turns red when indexing is stopped.
  5. From version 2025.6 the same start and stop control is also on the Transcript Tool Fast Menu, if you prefer to reach it from there.
Stop, not Pause. The two commands look interchangeable and are not. Pause All Indexing is a temporary hold and indexing resumes by itself. Stop PhraseFind AI Indexing holds until a person restarts it. If you want the machine quiet for the rest of the shift, Stop is the only one that does that.
This one is per project. Stopping the indexer applies to the project you have open, so on a station that opens a different project every shift you must repeat it, or set it once as a site setting as described below.
While you are in there. The same gear menu holds Manage Transcripts, which lets you delete transcripts already written for a project and reclaim the space. There is also a Search Data Directory setting, which controls where index data is written. Switching it from Default to Local Default keeps that traffic off shared storage.
Background
services

Stop Background Services, and stop them coming back

Transcode, consolidate, render and watch folders all run through a service that lives outside the application. Because it is a separate program, quitting Media Composer does not stop it, and it will happily keep working through a queue while you think the machine is idle.

There are two ways in, and they control the same service. The taskbar route works even when Media Composer is closed. The in application route is the one that matters here, because only that window holds the checkboxes that decide whether the service starts again tomorrow morning.

  1. Before stopping anything, look at what is queued. Click the Avid Background Services Manager icon in the Windows taskbar or the macOS menu bar and choose Open Background Queue Window. Avid will warn you that stopping the service kills a job in progress, so check first whether a colleague queued an overnight transcode.
  2. In Media Composer, go to Tools then Background Services. The Background Services window opens.
  3. Click Stop. The services halt immediately.
  4. Clear Always Start Avid Background Services at Launch. This is the one that stops the service coming back every time you open Media Composer.
  5. Tick Always Stop Avid Background Services upon Exit, so nothing is left chewing CPU after you go home.
  6. Click OK. The status now reads Inactive.

What each control in that window does

ControlWhat it does
StartImmediately starts the background services if they are currently stopped.
StopImmediately stops the background services if they are currently running.
PausePauses the services for a set length of time. If the pause runs out while you are playing, Avid extends it. Quitting the application clears the pause and the services resume.
Always Start at LaunchStarts the services whenever Media Composer opens. Clear this one.
Always Stop upon ExitStops the services when you quit Media Composer. Tick this one.
Enable Pausing of Background ServiceLets the services pause automatically during critical operations, which Avid lists as playback, capture and use of an Artist surface.
The setting almost nobody knows about. If your room genuinely needs background transcode and you cannot simply switch the service off, tick Enable Pausing of Background Service and leave it ticked. The service then steps out of the way by itself during playback and capture, which is exactly when stutter hurts most in a newsroom. It is the middle setting between losing the feature and losing your playback.
If the label reads differently, you are still in the right place. Avid renamed Avid Editor Services Manager to Avid Background Services Manager, and its own documentation still uses both names in the same document. Depending on your version the checkboxes may say Editor Services or Background Services. Same controls.
Do not leave this off and forget. If you later tick the background option in a Consolidate or Transcode dialog, the job will simply sit in the queue and never run. When somebody reports that a transcode is stuck, this is usually why.
Make it
stick

Site Settings, so every new project starts quiet

Everything above is per project or per session, which is why the problem keeps coming back. Media Composer solves this with Site Settings. When the application opens a new project it reads the Site Settings file first and loads whatever it finds there, and only then loads the project and user settings that were not already covered. Put your transcript choices in Site Settings and every project created on that machine inherits them.

  1. Set up one project exactly the way you want it, with the transcript settings above already applied.
  2. Open the Settings tab in the Project window.
  3. Select the settings you want to make permanent, then copy them into the Site Settings window.
  4. The Site Settings file lives in the Settings folder at the root of the Media Composer installation. Copy that one file to the other workstations and they all behave the same.
This is the answer to doing it once. For a room of edit suites, a single Site Settings file distributed by the engineering team beats twelve editors each remembering to click a gear.
The
nuclear
option

Physically remove the index plug-in

If you want a guarantee rather than a setting, you can take the plug-in out of the application so there is nothing left to run. Media Composer loads its AVX2 plug-ins from a folder on disk, and a plug-in that is not in that folder cannot be loaded. Quit Media Composer completely before you start.

  1. On macOS. Go to Applications and find Avid Media Composer. Right click AvidMediaComposer.app and choose Show Package Contents, then open Contents, SharedSupport, AVX2_Plug-ins.
  2. On Windows. Go to C:\Program Files\Avid\Avid Media Composer\AVX2_Plug-ins.
  3. Find the file named PhoneticIndexNN.avx.
  4. Drag it to your desktop rather than deleting it. That is your backup, and putting it back is how you undo this.
  5. Relaunch Media Composer. The plug-in is gone and cannot consume anything.
Three things to know before you do this. First, it is a whole machine change, not a project change, so it affects every editor who sits down at that station. Second, a Media Composer update or repair will usually put the file back, so this is not permanent in the way people expect and the station needs rechecking after every upgrade. Third, this file is the phonetic index plug-in, which is the engine behind the older phonetic PhraseFind rather than the newer AI transcription, so on a current release you should treat the Find window and Background Services steps above as the real fix and this as a belt and braces extra.
Cleaner alternative on a facility. If the room never uses dialogue search at all, simply not licensing the PhraseFind AI option achieves the same thing with none of the file surgery, and it survives updates.
At the
operating
system

The last resort, and when not to use it

If a station still shows background activity after everything above, the remaining levers are at system level. These are heavier, they are the ones most likely to cause a support call later, and on a facility machine they belong to the engineering team rather than to you.

  1. Startup items. On Windows open Task Manager and use the Startup tab to stop Avid Link and Background Services launching at login. On macOS use System Settings and Login Items.
  2. Services. On Windows, services.msc lists the Avid services and lets you set a startup type of Manual instead of Automatic. Manual is far safer than Disabled, because the service can still be brought up when something genuinely needs it.
  3. Licensing. If the PhraseFind AI option is not activated on the machine at all, the indexer has nothing to do. On a facility that never uses dialogue search, not licensing the option is the cleanest possible fix.
Read this before touching anything in this box. On a workstation connected to NEXIS, Interplay or MediaCentral, the Media Indexer is how shared media is found, and stopping it will make media appear to go offline. Avid Link is part of licence checking on subscription and floating licences, so removing it can stop Media Composer launching at all. Removing a plug-in from the application folder will usually be undone by the next update. None of this should be done on a shared edit suite without your systems engineer agreeing first.

Part three

Check that it actually worked

Do not trust a menu click. Three quick checks tell you whether the machine is genuinely quiet.

CheckWhereWhat quiet looks like
Indexer state Edit > Find The indicator beside PhraseFind AI Index is red, meaning indexing is stopped.
Bin state Bin Container sidebar The bins you excluded read Transcription Off in the column beside the name.
Background queue Taskbar or menu bar icon The Background Queue Window is empty and the service reports stopped.
Services status Tools > Background Services Status reads Inactive. The taskbar icon is grey, which means stopped. Green means active and orange means paused.
Watch folder activity Timeline progress indicator Grey means the background services are suspended. A spinning icon means processing is still happening.
Processes, Windows Task Manager No sustained CPU from AvidBackgroundServicesManager.exe or the transcode engine while you are not exporting.
Processes, macOS Activity Monitor Search for Avid and confirm nothing is holding CPU when Media Composer is sitting still.
Real world test Your timeline Import a fresh batch of rushes and scrub immediately. Before the change the machine hesitates, after it should not.

Part four

Putting it back

Nothing here is destructive and every step reverses. If a documentary job arrives where searching an interview by word is genuinely worth the CPU, turn it back on for that project and leave the rest of the room alone.

To undoDo this
Indexing stoppedEdit > Find, gear icon, then Start PhraseFind AI Indexing. Indexing resumes and works through the backlog.
Bin excludedRight click the bin and choose Include Bin in Transcription.
New bins not enrolledTranscript Settings, tick Include all new bins in transcription again.
Background Services stoppedTools > Background Services, then Start. Or click the taskbar or menu bar icon and choose Start.
Services no longer start at launchTools > Background Services, tick Always Start Avid Background Services at Launch again. If the taskbar icon has vanished entirely, this is also how you get it back, after a restart of the application.
Plug-in removedQuit Media Composer, put PhoneticIndexNN.avx back in the AVX2_Plug-ins folder, and relaunch.
Transcripts deletedThey regenerate from the media, so this costs processing time but no data. Use Manage Transcripts to see what exists.
Site Settings changedRemove the copied settings from the Site Settings window, and new projects go back to Avid's defaults.
